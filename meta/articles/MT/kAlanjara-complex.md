The earliest shaiva presence at the kAla~njara shaiva complex was most likely the atimArga tradition of the pashupata-s. There are several lines of evidence that support this contention: 

1. The ur-skandapurANa, which mentions this site, is clearly pAshupata in its primary affiliation. 
2. There are several depictions of lakulIsha and shaiva Acharya-s shown worshipping him. 
3. Inscriptions associated with the depictions of lakulIsha and the AchArya-s clearly mention the worship of lakulIsha. 
4. There are inscriptions with names of AchArya-s like bhAva-someshvara from 1100s, which are clearly indicative of them being pAshupata-s. 

## mantramArga
However, right from the gupta age, the complex has several dedications of female votaries and householders with their one or more wives depicted in the act of personal worship. This suggests that they had received dIkSha. 

By definition, the atimArga tradition was an ascetic one, and there is no evidence that the classical lakulIsha pAshupata-s offered dIkSha to women or even householders with one or more wives. This suggests that these votaries were affiliated with the bhairavIya/kaula or saiddhAntika shaiva tradition (both of which have female and householder initiates) rather than the atimArga. However, the question arises as to whether any of these were active at kAla~njara complex. 

Emphatic evidence for the saiddhAntika presence in the kala~njara complex is offered by the opening verse of the scripture of this tradition, the raurava-sUtra-saMgraha:

> kAleshvaram mahAkAlaM  
kAla~njara-nivAsinam |  
kAlakR^it-kAlavettAraM  
praNato .asmi sadAshivam ||

> I bow down to the lord of time, to the great time (mahAkAla), to the dweller on kAla~njara (as an aside, the base of the kAla~njara mountain has one of the oldest rocks in India), to the creator of time, the knower of time, to sadAshiva.

Consistent with this, kAla~njara is one of the pa~nchAShTaka (5 x 8) pilgrimage sites of the saiddhAntika tradition, coming within the sthAnvaShTaka group.

We also believe that evidence in the affirmative of the saiddhAntika presence is offered by an inscription from the fort, dated Thursday, January 10, 1090 CE in the secular calendar. Here, shrImUrti, the rAjaguru of chandella kIrtivarman, records the construction of a maNDapa for the shaiva complex. It separately mentions shaiva-s and pAshupata, with a certain Acharya vArika as their chief being involved in the process. We take shaiva in this inscription to specifically mean saiddhAntika shaiva-s. However, from the inscription, it is possible that both groups had some symbiosis and fronted a common chief (pramukha) as representative.

In light of the above, we interpret one unique image of shiva from cave 4 of the kala~njara shaiva complex as support for saiddhAntika presence. Here a 9-headed and 10-handed shiva is depicted flanked by shaiva AchArya-s and a votary engaged in worship. ASI has interpreted the image as mahAsadAshiva. We agree it is sadAshiva, but there is no evidence for a 9-headed mAhAsadAshiva. 

We hold this form to be an anthropomorphic depiction of the hallowed 9-fold maNDala, based on the vyomavyApin mantra with its 9 x 9 structure, taught by the Kashmirian mantravAdin bhaTTa rAmakaNTha-II and his southern successors: ekAshitipadam devaM nava-parvokti-darshanAt || we suggest that the nine parvan-s of the mantra and maNDala are depicted here as the nine heads of sadAshiva.
