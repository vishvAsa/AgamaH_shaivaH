Also, one should know how to imply words so that the mantra makes sense. The words “punar udayat” have an implied temporal duration built into it—the Ṛṣi's lifetime, lokatrayapralaya, avāntara/madhyamapralaya or mahāpralaya. 

On a separate note, this is similar to how the words, “idam agre” (this [cosmos] in the beginning…) in Śruti are often followed by different terms: sat, asat and an exclusion of both or other things. If one takes it all literally, it will be nothing but nonsense. 

In each case, the idea is to start a particular narrative or explanation at an appropriate starting point so as to emphasize the importance of a specific prior state of the world. The one that describes the prior condition of the world/cosmos as neither sat nor asat (which is explained in the Veda as referring to the mind) is starting the story with the prior state of the cosmos where it laid in a latent form in manotattva. 

Another that starts with Sat is giving importance to the positive material cause. Yet another that starts with Asat (identified with primordial Ṛṣis in the form of prāṇas) is emphasizing a different starting point in the pre-cosmic story, where the world was in a state of Prāṇas animated by the primordial Ṛṣis.
