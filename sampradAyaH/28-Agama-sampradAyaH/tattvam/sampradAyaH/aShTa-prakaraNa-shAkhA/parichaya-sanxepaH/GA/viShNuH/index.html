viShNus

A Saiddhāntika cannot hate Vișņu. There are at least 3 levels of Vișņu (in my estimation, 4) in Saiddhāntika metaphysics & each of them has an importance for us.

Roles

The lowest Vișņu is himself one of the saptaguru-s.
One of the higher Vișņus is someone an Ācārya has to worship in Dīkșā and offer the Dīkșita’s sensory faculties to him as ‘sulka’ for purification.
A yet higher one is one of Āvaraņadevatas in the Caturthāvaraņa, worshiped in the daily Pañcāvaraņa-Pūjā.

The yet higher one (the highest Vișņu) is essentially non-different from Paramaśiva and is identical to one of His 3 Śaktis (see chit-shakti note). Vișņu at the highest level is not a soul but a Śakti of Paramaśiva. I am not saying: this highest viSNu is a soul that is non-different from paramashiva (who is a distinct soul). The other three viSNu-s are separate souls.

Post attainment

To make it clear, a soul can do appropriate sAdhana and can attain the post of the 3 lower viSNus but the 4th and highest viSNu is not something that can be attained as it is innate to paramashiva.

Basis

There are four Viśṇus, in my humble opinion.

Notwithstanding questions of attribution of the cited text and other academic concerns, how I would theologically interpret the text you have cited, presuming that it is indeed from Aghoraśiva’s Kriyākramadyotikā:

I will apply Ācārya Rāmakaṇṭha’s rule from His Sārdhatriśatikālottara-Vṛtti:

“न चेह पशवः पाशाश् चेज्यन्ते”

“Here (इह / iha i.e. in this Siddhānta system), Paśus & Pāśas (पशवः पाशाश्च / paśavah pāśāśca) are not (na / न) worshiped (इज्यन्ते / ijyante).”

“Here (in this siddhānta), Paśus & Pāśas are not worshiped.”

Identification - triad

Citation

Mahotsavavidhi in Aghora-Śivācārya’s Shaiva ritual paddhati

धरणि-सलिल-दहन-पवन-गगन-गन्ध-रस-रूप-स्पर्श-शब्दादि-भाव-मेदादिभेद-भिन्न-विविध-प्रपञ्चात्मक-महेश-कृपा-निर्दिष्ट-सकल-भुवन-रक्षण-विचक्षणः, शङ्ख-चक्र-गदा-खड्गादि-दिव्यायुध-अलङ्कृत-कर-कमल-विराजमानः, त्रिविक्रमक्रान्त-सकल-भुवनतलः, निजोदर-पुट-विधृत-निखिलाण्ड-षण्डभर-नियमित-खर्वीकृत-दर्वीकर-गर्व-सर्वकष-पराक्रम-परिक्रान्त-सुदृढ-गरुड-वाहनः, मनोहरारविन्द-सुन्दर-नाभिमन्दिर-जनित-पितामहः, सकल-जलनिधि-निमग्न-काश्यपि-समुद्ररण-परिगृहीत-वराहरूपः, सकल-जगदाधार-भूत-विकट-कमठ-रूपः, मत्स्यावतार-विहार-सदन-महाम्बुधि-विशालोल्लोल-कल्लोलमाल-जल-कलित-विचित्र-चरितः, असह्य-नरसिंह-विक्रम-विकसित-नखर-शिखर-विदलित-हिरण्य-वक्षःस्थल-गलित-बहुल-रुधिर-धारा-सम्पादित-सन्ध्या-राग-रञ्जित-मरकत-शैल-बन्धूर-कन्धर-मनोहर-विग्रहः, अरविन्द-मन्दिरेन्द्र-कठिन-कुच-तट-पटिरपङ्किल-वक्षःस्थलः, जगदाधार-दण्ड-प्रकाण्ड-चण्डतर-चतुर्भुज-दण्डकन्दाः, परमेश्वराज्ञा-परिपालित-सकललोकः, शिवध्यान-योगामृत-पारण-परायणो, नारायणमूर्तिः शिवास्त्र-दिव्य-शूल-वाम-पत्रे सान्निध्यं करोतु ॥

May the manifestation of Lord Nārāyaṇa, who is expert in protecting all the worlds directed by the grace of Lord Maheśa (Śiva), a diverse universe differentiated by elements like earth, water, fire, wind, ether, smell, taste, form, touch, and sound; who is radiant with His lotus hands adorned with divine weapons such as the conch, discus, mace, and sword; who enveloped the entire universe as Trivikrama; whose firm mount, Garuḍa, traverses with an all-conquering valor that subdues the pride of serpents while the Lord holds the weight of all universes within His own belly; who gave birth to Brahmā from the temple of His beautiful lotus-navel; who assumed the form of the Boar (Varāha) to rescue the earth submerged in the cosmic ocean; who took the form of the gigantic Tortoise (Kūrma) to become the foundation of the entire universe; whose wondrous pastimes were enacted in the massive rolling waves of the great ocean that was the playground of His Fish (Matsya) incarnation; whose captivating form possesses a beautiful neck like an emerald mountain, reddened by a twilight-like hue from the streams of blood flowing from Hiraṇyakaśipu’s chest, torn apart by the sharp claws of the irresistible Narasiṃha; whose chest is smeared with sandalwood paste from the firm breasts of Goddess Lakṣmī (Indirā) who dwells in a lotus; whose four fierce arms are like massive pillars supporting the universe; who protects all realms in accordance with the command of the Supreme Lord (Parameśvara); and who is devoted to partaking in the nectar of yoga and meditation upon Lord Śiva – may He take His divine presence upon the left blade of Lord Śiva’s divine trident (Triśūla).

1st viShNu

This rule will exclude the 1st Viṣṇu. I think only those in Śuddhādhvā (those who have crossed over paśutvamala into adhikāramala or nirmalatva) are objects of yajana/arcana in the Saiddhāntika context.

2nd viShNu

It’s a little complicated to explain why I will not omit the 2nd Viṣṇu for this reason. I will delve into this some other time.

3rd viShNu

However, I will omit the 2nd and 3rd Viṣṇus because the Viṣṇu in the text cited (Mahotsavavidhi of Aghora-Śivācārya-paddhati) occurs in a triadic context (see very last line: śūlavāmapatre, referring to the three-pronged śūla),
while the 2nd Viṣṇu is part of a pentad
and 3rd is part of a decad.

4th viShNu

So, I pick the 4th and highest Viṣṇu, that is identified by Nirmalamaṇi-Deśika in his commentary as not just presiding over the Vidyātattva
(even here, I feel that there is an additional technicality, where the Vidyātattva is not merely a singular, collective reference to the śuddhāśuddha heptad of Vidyātattvas as if often is interpreted, but actually a reference to something more sublime; but I’ll let it rest here)
but also as being identical with Śiva’s Kriyāśakti. This highest Viṣṇu is not a separate soul like the lower three but a phase of Śiva’s own Śakti.

avatAra-s?

In this context, the reference to avatāras may be anomalous. But one can read this as either referring to those avatāras in the pure or impure adhvās—in either case, the highest Viṣṇu is rhetorically identified with them because it is impelling those avatāras.
