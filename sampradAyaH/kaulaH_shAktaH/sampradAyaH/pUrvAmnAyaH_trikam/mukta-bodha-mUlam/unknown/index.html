    • description: Description by Mark S.G. Dyczkowski: The text, which is unnamed, is copied is a part of a manuscript that begins with Aśeṣakulavallarī. I have called it Parākramapujā. It tells us that it belongs to the tradition called Aśeṣāmbhavīmata. Parā, the goddess worshipped here, is also called Nityā. The worship takes places both internally and externally. The inner processes are described in detail. As usual the concern is with the flows of vital force and Kuṇḍalinī. The offering of wine and other details confirm the Kaula character of the outer worship. The text supplies an extensive and numerous Mantras from which the close association with the śrīvidyā school is clear, although śrīcakra is not the object of worship.

    • catalog number: M00310
    • description: The Devīrahasya and Uddhārakośa are published together in one volume. Edited by Rāma candra Kāka and Haribhaṭṭa śāstrī, it was published originally published in Kashmir in 1941. The Devīrahasya is considered to be a part of the Rudrayāmala. It appears to be have been composed in Kashmiri as it refers repeatedly to the goddesses Jvālāmukhī, śārikā, Mahārājñī, śāradā, Bhidā, and Bālā who have shrines in the Kathmandu Valley where they were very popular. The words Arabic words phirangahā and safak found on pages 307 and 317 indicate that it was composed when the Muslims were already establized in the Valley. The Devīrahasya is divided into three parts, as follows. A) The Pūrvārdha (First Half). This consists of twenty-five paṭalas that deal with (1) Initiation. (2) Mantras of form so the Goddess. (3) Mantras of śiva. (4) Mantras of Viṣṇu. (5) Utkelana or unlocking of Mantras. (6) Vitalizing of Mantras. (7) Methods to remove curses attached to Mantras. (8) The mental utterance of Mantras (pārāyaṇa). (9) How to use particular seed syllables (bīja) uttered , before and after particular Mantras. (10) Preliminary practice of Mantras. (11) The sacrificial rite performed for the preliminary practice of Mantras. (12) Diagrams used for worship (yantra). (13) How to wear amulets containing those diagrams. (14) The Rṣ.i, metre etc of Mantras. (15, 16) Worship in cremation grounds. (17, 18) Purification of the impliments used for worship. (19) The origin of wine. (20) Ritual ingestion of wine. (21) Prayers and hymns dedicated to śākta sādhakas (vīra). (22) Purification of wine. (23) Purification of the consort. (24) Different kinds of rosary and their purification. (25) Purification etc., of the diagrams. B) The uttarārdha (Second Half) otherwise called Rahasyavijaya consists of thirty-five chapters embodying the Pañcāṅgas, Paṭala, Kavaca, Paddhati Sahasranāma and Stotra of Gaṇeśa, Sūrya, Lakṣhmīnārāyaṇa, śiva and Durgā. The appendix (Pariśiṣṭha) gives the Pañcāṅgas of Jvālāmukhī, śārikā, Mahārajñī and Bālā the family goddesses of the Kashmiri paṇḍits. B) The Uddhārakośa (Treasure of Mantras) which is in the appendix. Written in the form of a dialogue between Dakṣināmūrti and his disciple Akṣobhya, it is a collection of quotations from no less than forty-seven Tantrika works. It is in seven sections. 1) The Mantras of the goddesses Tripurā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Tārā, Bhuvaneśvarī, Mātaṅgī, śarikā, Rāñjī, Bhīdā and Jvālāmukhī along with some seed syllable Mantras and their names. 2) The Mantras of Bhadrakālī, Turī, chinnamastā, Dakṣiṇākālikā, śyāmā and Kālarātrī. 3) The Mantras of Vajrayoginī, Vārāhī, śāradā, Kāmeśvarī, Gaurī, Annapurṇā and Kulvāgīśvarī along with those of Ganeśa, Vatuka, Kumāra, Mṛtyuñjaya, Kārtaviryārjuna, Sugrīva and Hanuman. 4) The Mantras of the nine planets. 5) Names of the letters of the Sanskrit alphabet. 6) The Mantras of Bhavānī, Bagalāmukhī, Indrākṣī and Khecarī. 7) Descriptions of the goddesses, gods and planets mentioned in the preceding sections.
