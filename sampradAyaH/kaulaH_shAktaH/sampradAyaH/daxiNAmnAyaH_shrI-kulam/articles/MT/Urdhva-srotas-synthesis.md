An interesting case of synthesis between the Urdhvasrotas and the shrIkula tradition is seen in the great bhAskararAya makhIndra's visualization of the shrIkula goddess' body as composed of the saiddhAntika Agama-s:

kāmikaṃ pāda-kamalaṃ yogajaṃ gulphayor yugam |
pāda-dvayāṅgulī-rūpe kāraṇa-prasṛtāhvaye ||
The Kāmika Āgama (1) is Her lotus feet; the Yogaja (2) is Her pair of ankles. The Kāraṇa (3) and the one called Prasṛta (4) take the form of the toes of Her two feet.

ajitā jānunor yugmaṃ dīptam ūru-dvayaṃ vibhoḥ |
pṛṣṭha-bhāge 'ṃśumān asya nābhiḥ śrī-suprabhedakam ||

The Ajitā (5) is the pair of knees of the omnipresent Goddess; the Dīpta (6) is Her two thighs. The Aṃśumat (7) constitutes Her back, and the holy Suprabheda (8) is Her navel.

vijayaṃ jaṭharaṃ prāhuḥ niḥśvāsaṃ hṛdayātmakam |
svāyambhuvaṃ stana-dvandvaṃ analaṃ locana-trayam ||
They declare the Vijaya (9) to be Her abdomen; the Niḥśvāsa (10) is Her heart. The Svāyambhuva (11) is Her pair of breasts, and the Anala (12) is Her three eyes.

vīrāgamaḥ kaṇṭhadeśo ruru-tantraṃ śruti-dvayam |
mukuṭaṃ mukuṭaṃ tantraṃ bāhavo vimalāgamaḥ ||
The Vīrāgama (13) is Her neck region; the Ruru-tantra is (14) Her two ears. The Mukuṭa-tantra (15) is Her crown, and the Vimalāgama (16) constitutes Her arms.

candro jñānam uraḥ proktaṃ bimbaṃ vadana-paṅkajam |
prodgīta-tantraṃ rasanā lalitaṃ gaṇdayor yugam ||
The Candrajñāna (17) is declared to be Her chest; the Bimba (18) is Her lotus face. The Prodgītatantra (19) is Her tongue, and the Lalita (20) is Her pair of cheeks.

siddha lalāṭa-phalakaṃ santānaṃ kuṇḍala-dvayam |
kiraṇaṃ ratna-bhūṣā syāt vātulaṃ vasanātmakam ||
The Siddha (21) is Her broad forehead; the Santāna (22) is Her pair of earrings. The Kiraṇa (23) is Her jeweled ornaments, and the Vātula (24) constitutes Her garments.

āṅgopāṅgāni romāṇi tantrāṇyanyāni kṛtsnaśaḥ |
evaṃ tantrātmakaṃ rūpaṃ mahādevyā vicintayet ||
Her major and minor limbs, as well as Her body hairs, are entirely all the remaining tantras. In this manner, one should meditate upon the form of the Mahādevī, which is composed of the Tantras.
