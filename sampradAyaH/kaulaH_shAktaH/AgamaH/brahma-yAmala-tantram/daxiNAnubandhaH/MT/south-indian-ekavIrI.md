The installation of this goddess ekavIrI, an ectype of chAmuNDA, indicates that in the high point of the choLa-s, a millennium ago, worship was being performed as per the South Indian supplement of brahmayAmala tantra. This goddess is a unique hallmark of that tantra:

> sarvāṅgaṃ tu nyasen mantrī  
ekavīrī-śarīrataḥ  |  
brāhmī māheśvarī caiva   
kaumārī vaiṣṇavī tathā ||  
vārāhī caiva māhendrī    
vīrabhadra-gaṇādhipau |  
ekavīrī śarīre tu  
vinyased vaikamudrayā ||

> The mantrin should perform nyāsa on all the limbs, [projecting them] from the body of Ekavīrī. Brāhmī, Māheśvarī, and likewise Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī,
Vārāhī and Māhendrī, and [the pair] Vīrabhadra and Gaṇādhipa.
[All these] he should place within the body of Ekavīrī, or [alternatively do the nyāsa] with the single mudrā. (South Indian "brahma-yAmala")

In this tradition, ekavIrI (=chAmuNDA) occupies the central position among the mAtR^ikA-s.

> It's a mantrification of the moment of the withdrawal of the Goddesses back into mahAsarasvatI from the saptashati ... I heard of this Goddess by the name of Nishumbha-shudini.

owever, it should be noted that in the South Indian brahmayAmala tradition the emergence of ekavIrI is placed in the context of the dAruka-vadha episode; itself inherited from the koTivarSha narrative from your regions:

Generation of the mAtR^i-s as per the South Indian brahmayAmala (minimally emended from manuscript):
