On the South Indian Brahma-yāmala supplement

The South Indian Brahma-yāmala supplement (hereinafter, BY-SI) has 51 chapters (paṭala-s), of which 49 have come down to us, albeit in a somewhat corrupt and lacunose fashion. The 51st chapter is incomplete. The BY-SI chapters show variations in their colophons. The most common one takes the form:

  • brahma-yāmale śata-sahasra-saṃhitāyām <X>-nāma <N> paṭalaḥ |

This suggests that it saw itself as part of a greater “Brahma-yāmala” of a 100,000 verses. Keeping with this, internally, the BY-SI explicitly associates its ritualists with the knowers of the Brahma-yāmala:

mūrtipair lakṣaṇopetair aṣṭabhis tu yathā vidhi |
mantrajñaiḥ kuśalaiś śuddhair brahma-yāmala-tatparaiḥ ||
[The installation is done] according to the sacred rules, by the eight tenders of the image, indeed, who are endowed with the prescribed qualifications, who know the mantra-s, are highly skilled, ritually pure, and knowers of the Brahma-yāmala.

The less common variants include:

  • brahma-yāmale vidyāpīṭhāvatārite pratiṣṭhā-tantre ekāśīti-vidhānāṅge mātṛ-śānti-vidhir nāma aṣṭādaśa-paṭalaḥ ||

This long colophon explicitly associates the text with the Vidyāpīṭha class of tantra-s and further specifies it to be a pratiṣṭha-tantra. This aligns with its connections to the Mātṛ-sadbhāva from the Cera country, another text associated with the Brahma-yāmala tradition. Within the pratiṣṭhā-tantra, it talks of a vidhāna of 81 parts. The root text it is referring to here remains unknown.

  • brahma-yāmale utsava-vidhāne dundubhiḥ praharaṇaṃ nāma sapta-daśa-paṭalaḥ ||

Here, it again refers to a vidhāna pertaining to festivals, whose source text remains unknown.
