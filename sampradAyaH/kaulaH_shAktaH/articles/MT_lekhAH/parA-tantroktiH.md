## The pUrvAmnAya in the parA-tantra
Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2066039566570594403)

The parA-tantra teaches the ShaD-AmnAya system, which adds two additional AmnAya-s to the original four that comprise the archaic directional tetrad. It distinguishes as the ultimate AmnAya (UrdhvAmnAya) the worship of tripura-bhairavI or tripura-sundarI in the shrI-chakra from the goddess of the dakShiNAmnAya. It incorporates the vajrayANa goddesses in the adhAmnAya and salvages some of the bauddha traditions into the Hindu fold.  In our opinion, it remembers some unique features of a transmission of the pUrvAmnAya distinct from that outlined in the chi~nchiNI-mata-sAra-samuchchaya, and expounded in the mAlinI-vijaya and trika. However, it is probably aligned to a degree with that of the siddhayogeshvarI-mata. The central goddess of the AmnAya, pUrNeshvarI, is described thus:

pUrNeshvarI mahogrA sA pUrvAmnAya-prakIrtitA |
chakreshvaryAs tu vakShyAmi sAdhakAnAM hitAya vai || 55 ||
She, pUrNeshvarI, intensely fierce, is celebrated in the Eastern Transmission. Now, for the ultimate benefit of the practitioners, I shall describe the mistress of the chakra.

dhyAnaM tu chitte .adhiShThAya dhyAtavyA parameshvarI |
indra-nIla-samaprakhyAM dasha-dor-daNDa-dhAriNIm || 56 ||
Establishing this visualization firmly within the consciousness, one should meditate upon the Supreme Goddess. She possesses a complexion resembling a deep blue sapphire and wields ten staff-like arms.

varAbhayakaraM ramyaM satrishUlaM kapAlakam |
DamaruM chAkShamAlAM cha shaktIM chaiva karAmbuje || 57 ||
In her lotus-like hands, she holds beautiful gestures granting boons and fearlessness, a trident, a skull, a hand-drum, a rosary, and a spear.

ikShu-chApAM sharAM chaiva khaDga-hastAM sushobhinIm |
mahApretopari tatra chandra-sUryAsanAsthitAM || 58 ||
She also holds a sugarcane bow, arrows, and brandishes a sword, appearing supremely beautiful. She stands atop a great corpse, seated upon a throne composed of the sun and the moon.

pratyAlIDhAM trinetrAM cha muNDa-mAlA-vibhUShitAM |
vAsukI-sha~NkhapAlau tau karNa-bhUShaNatAM gatau || 59 ||
She is poised in the attacking stance (pratyAlIDhA), has three eyes, and is adorned with a garland of severed heads. The great serpents vAsukI and sha~NkhapAla serve as her earrings.

ananta-hAra-mAlA cha hArAlaMkAra-bhUShitAM |
vishAla-traya-netrAM cha chandrArddha-kR^ita-shekharAm || 60 ||
She is adorned with garlands, including a  garland made of the serpent ananta, possesses three wide eyes, and carries the crescent moon upon her crest.

eShA pUrNeshvarI devI hAhArAva-ravapriyA |
pUrNeshvarIti vikhyAtA sAhaMkArA rajoyutA || 61 ||
This is the Goddess pUrNeshvarI, who is deeply fond of the terrifying, roaring sound of hAhA. Renowned as pUrNeshvarI, she is imbued with cosmic ego and is charged with dynamic energy.

girau pUrNAlaye pIThe lakShmI-vana-shmashAnake |
indreNArAdhitA nityA bahurUpAvatAriNI || 62 ||
Upon the mountain at the sacred seat of pUrNAlaya (pUrNagiri), in the cemetery lakShmI-vana, she is eternally worshipped by indra – the manifestation of the bahurUpA R^ik (aghorebhyo .atha...)

pUrvAmnAyeti vikhyAtA pUrva-devarShipUjitA |
IshvarI sarva-vidyAnAM mahA-bhAgya-bharAvahA || 63 ||
Renowned as the personification of the Eastern Transmission, she was worshipped by the gods and seers of old. She is the absolute sovereign of all knowledge (mantra-s), the great bearer and vehicle fortune.

nAnA-rUpa-dharA nityA bahurUpAvatAriNI |
jayA jayantI vijayA ajitA nArasiMhikA || 64 ||
vyAghreshvarI karAlA cha rAjeshI bhuvaneshvarI |
raktadantA vishAlAkShI kAmeshI chaNDamAlinI || 65 ||
vidyujjihvA DAmareshI nATeshI nR^ityanAyikA |
nandI chaiva mahAkAla-bhR^i~NgiNI cha kulAlikA || 66 ||
She assumes diverse forms, is the nityA, and the manifestation of the bahurUpA R^ik. The following 20 devI-s (her manifold emanations) represent her retinue:
1. jayA, 2. jayantI,  3. vijayA,  4. ajitA,  5. nArasiMhikA, 6. vyAghreshvarI, 7. karAlA, 8. rAjeshI, 9. bhuvaneshvarI, 10. raktadantA,  11. vishAlAkShI, 12. kAmeshI, 13 chaNDamAlinI, 14. vidyujjihvA, 15. DAmareshI, 16. nATeshI, 17. nR^itya-nAyikA, 18.
   nandI, 19. mahAkAla-bhR^i~NgiNI, 20. kulAlikA

nava-nATeshvarI tatra chatuH-pa~ncha-rasAnvitA |
bahubhedA devadevI bhairavAShTaka-mAtR^ikAH || 67 ||
In their midst, she is the Nine-fold Queen of the Dance, imbued with 4+5 rasa-s.  Of many varieties, the supreme goddess,  with the eight bhairava-s and mAtR^i-s.

nATeshvarIti vikhyAtA bhairavI chakra-pAlikA |
kShetra-vighnesha-baTuka-yoginI-mAtR^ikAShTakAH || 68 ||
Celebrated as the Mistress of the Dance, the bhairavI and the protectress of chakra, she is accompanied by kShetrapAla, vighnesha, baTuka, the yoginI-s and the eight mAtR^ikA-s.


## dakShiNAmnAya as per the parA-tantra

Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2066397826867704029)

While in the old kulAmnAya system had the shrI-kula goddess kAmeshvarI as the mistress of the dakShiNAmnAya, with her being made the central deity (as tripurA) of the UrdhvAmnAya in the ShaDAmnAya system, the dakShiNAmnAya is occupied by a distinct fierce goddess nisheshvarI who is iconographically equivalent to chAmuNDA rather than the shrIkula goddess. Indeed, she is called rakta-charchikA and rakta-chAmuNDA(I). She is also repeatedly termed chaitanya-bhairavI and nityA. While associated with the crematory fire, she is interestingly enthroned on the throne of the four veda-s. Her consort is implicitly said to be aghora bhairava. Her retinue features 24 deities, including nIlA-sarasvatI, dakShiNa-kAlikA, some mahAvidyA-s and the ancient kaumAra-mAtR^i-s, like jambhinI etc (who are also part of vArAhI's retinue). Below is the section from the parA-tantra on nisheshvarI and the dakShiNAmnAya as per the ShaDAmnAya krama.

dakShiNAmnAya vakShyAmi mahAvIryA maholbaNA |
aghora-bhairavoddiShTA nisheshI cha nira~NgalA || 1 ||
Now I shall describe the Southern Transmission, O one endowed with great virility and boundlessly vast. This stream was revealed by aghora bhairava [has as its] supreme goddess nisheshI (The Queen of the Night), who is completely unfettered.

nirAlambeshvarI nityA aghoraM tach ChiraM viduH |
siMhAsana-gatA nityA nisheshI rakta-charchikA || 2 ||
"She is the Sovereign Ruler who requires no external support, eternal, and the wise know that her crest/head is aghora [bhairava]. She is eternally seated upon a supreme lion-throne – she, nisheshI is rakta-charchikA.

chaitanya-bhairavI kechid raktakAlIti kechana |
sundarI kAlikA kechid aghoreshvarI kechana || 3 ||
Some call her chaitanya-bhairavI, while others call her raktakAlI. Some address her as sundarI and kAlikA, and still others call her aghoreshvarI.

dhyAna-rUpaM tu vakShyAmi bhairavyA yAmya-dik-sthitA |
nityA niraMshani-ratA nisheshI nirguNAshrayA || 4 ||
Now I shall state the visualization of this bhairavI who presides over the quarter of yama. She is eternal, delighting in undivided reality, the queen of the night, who is the foundation of the quality-less absolute.

kratunArAdhitA devi dharmarAjAya vai purA |
sampradAya-kareNaiva devI chaitanya-bhairavI || 5 ||
In ancient times, she was propitiated by a sacrifice for the sake of yama, the good of justice. Through that very line of master-to-disciple lineage transmission, the goddess chaitanya-bhairavI [became manifest].

chatur-vedAsanA devI mahA-phetkAra-saMsthitA |
prajvalantI chitA-madhye dAkShiNya-dakShadA parA || 6 ||
The Goddess sits upon a throne composed of the four veda-s, yet she is established amidst the terrifying, howls [of jackals and ghosts]. Blazing in the center of the funeral pyre, she is the supreme bestower of harmony and skill."

alAta-chakravad yasyA bhramanti shira-mAlikA |
snAyur-akShA kR^ishA~NgI cha asthi-charmAvasheShitA || 7 ||
Her garland of severed heads rotates such that it appears like a continuous ring of fire from a whirling torch. With sinewy eyes, she is emaciated, reduced to [a structural lattice of] bare bone and skin.

vyAghra-charmA kaTi-vR^itA nara-charmottarIyakA |
sahasrAditya-saMkAshA tapta-kA~nchana-suprabhA || 8 ||
A tiger skin is wrapped around her hips, and a human skin serves as her upper garment. She is resplendent like a thousand suns with the glow of molten gold.

a~ncha-vaktrA chAtibhImA tri-pa~ncha-nayanair yutA |
IShad-dhAsya-lalaj-jihvA nimna-nAbhi-kR^ishodarI || 9 ||
"She possesses five faces (pa~ncha-vaktrA) and is exceedingly terrifying (atibhImA), endowed with thrice-five (fifteen) cosmic eyes (tri-pa~ncha-nayanaiH). Her tongue rolls and lolls out amidst a subtle, mocking laugh (IShad-dhAsya-lalaj-jihvA); she has a deeply indented navel and an incredibly slender, emaciated belly (nimna-nAbhi-kR^ishodarI)."

vikarAlA-sanasthAtA chakra-vaktrAsana-sthitA |
raudrI bhayApahA ghorA chandrArddha-kR^ita-ketanA || 10 ||
She is seated upon a terrifying throne, stationed on a seat composed of a wheel of visages. She is the shakti of rudra, the destroyer of fear, the terrible one, who bears the crescent moon as her banner.

barbarArddha-mukta-keshI nAga-rAja-virAjitA |
kabandha-uShNIya-yutA brahmA-shIrSha-shikhA-maNiH || 11 ||
"Her curly hair is half-loosened, resplendent with the kings of serpents. She wears a turban woven from headless trunks, and the severed head of brahman serves as her crest-jewel.

sadyash Chinna-jagad-raktaH shiro-mAlA-vibhUShitA |
bhujA-ShoDasha-saMyuktA gaja-ka~nchuka-ki~NkiNI || 12 ||
She is adorned with a garland of severed heads dripping with the fresh blood of the universe. She is endowed with sixteen arms, wearing a jacket made of an elephant's hide decorated with tinkling bells.

pAshA~Nkusha-kapAlaM cha khaTvA~NgaM muNDa-DiNDimaM |
khaDga-kheTa-bANa-dhanush-chakra-daNDa-kamaNDalum || 13 ||
abhayaM varadaM chaiva tarjanI vidhR^itA karaiH |
muditA siddhi-madhunA shaktibhiH parivAritA || 14 ||
In her hands, she holds a noose, a goad, a skull, a skull-topped rod, a drum made of a skull, a sword, a shield, an arrow, a bow, a chakra, a staff, and a water-pot. She displays the gestures of fearlessness and boon-bestowing, while her index finger is raised in the threatening gesture. She is joyful by the honey of siddhi-s and is surrounded by her retinue of shakti-s.

hUM-hUM-hUM-kAra-nAdena trAsayan devatA-gaNAn |
R^ig-yajuH-sAmAtharvAkhyA vedasyopari sthitA || 15 ||
She oppresses the host of deities with her intensive hUMkAra as she is stationed atop the fourfold veda, the R^ig, yajuH, sAma, and atharva.

shavarUpI-mahAdeva-hR^idayopari-saMsthitA |
nisheshI cha mahogrA cha dakShiNAchAra-varjitA || 16 ||
She is firmly stationed upon the heart of mahAdeva, in the form of a corpse. She is nisheshI, the very fierce one, and she has abandoned the right-handed path.

jyeShThAnujyeShTha-bhedAsti yAmya-siMhAsanAshritA |
tasyA~Nga-devatAH sarve bahu-bhedA maholbaNA || 17 ||
She who is seated upon the Southern Lion-Throne manifests in hierarchies of seniority and juniority. All her ancillary deities are of several types and are boundlessly vast.

jaMbhinI staMbhinI naramohinI andhinI shivA |
mR^ityu~njayA charchikA cha siMhI dakShiNa-kAlikA || 18 ||
prachaNDA ugrachaNDA cha ugratArA kapAlinI |
tvaritA Chinna-shIrShA cha bagalA vAk-pradAyinI || 19 ||
nIlA sarasvatI bhImA trailokya-DAmareshvarI |
trailokya-vijayA chaNDI rakta-chAmuNDA bhairavI || 20 ||
[They are:] 1. jaMbhinI,  2. staMbhinI,  3. naramohinI, 4. andhinI, 5. shivA, 6. mR^ityu~njayA, 7. charchikA, 8. siMhI, 9. dakShiNa-kAlikA, 10. prachaNDA, 11. ugrachaNDA, 12. ugratArA, 13. kapAlinI, 14. tvaritA. 15. Chinna-shIrShA, 16. bagalA, 17. vAk-pradAyinI, 18. nIlA-sarasvatI, 19. bhImA, 20. trailokya-DAmareshvarI, 21. trailokya-vijayA, 22. chaNDI, 23. rakta-chAmuNDA 24. bhairavI

dakShiNAmnAya-deveshI nAnA-bheda-visAriNI |
brahmAdyAShTa-bhairavAShTa-kShetrapAlAShTam eva cha || 21 ||
sva-kShetra-nAthaM baTukaM gaNeshaM yoginI-gaNAn |
ekA vibhinnarUpA sA samayI pAlanAya cha || 22 ||
The Supreme Goddess of the Southern Transmission expands in diverse modalities. Though she is absolute Unity, she emanates these diverse forms for the protection of the sacred lineage: the eight bhairava-s headed by brahman (the "bhairavized" deities), the eight kShetrapAla-s, the octad (of mother goddesses), the specific lord of her own sacred site (sva-kShetra-nAtha), baTuka, gaNesha, and the host of yoginI-s. 

aghorasya gaNAH sarve nAnA-pAriShadAvR^itA |
mahA-chaNDATTa-saMrAva pUrayanti digantarAH || 23 ||
vetAlA bahulA tatra DAkinI-gaNa-saMvR^itAH |
bAla-lIlA-samApannA nishAchara-gaNAvR^itaH || 24 ||
All the hosts of aghora surround her as diverse regiments of attendants. They fill all directions with the thunderous roars of their terrifying laughter. In that space wander innumerable vetAla-s, surrounded by hosts of DAkinI-s; enveloped by legions of rakShas-es, they engage in child-like play.

daNDa-pAshodyata-karAH sarva-sAdhaka-siddhidA |
shAka-puShkara-dvIpeShu sAdhakA-bheda-darshinI || 25 ||
chauhAre gahvare pIThe yoni-maNDala-mArga-gA |
vAmA-chAra-ratA pUjyA hy aNimAdy aShTa-siddhidA || 26 ||
With hands raised high, wielding a staff and a noose, they bestow siddhi-s on practitioners. Even across the continents of shAka (Iranic lands) and puShkara, she reveals herself to different types of practitioners. Within the grove sanctuary of the sacred seat of chauhAra (near Shimla, HP?), she is attained by those practicing that path of the yoni-maNDala. Delighting in the transgressive left-handed path, she is to be worshipped as the ultimate bestower of the eight siddhi-s beginning with aNimA.
